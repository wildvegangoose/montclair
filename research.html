Montclair Public Library & Montclair History Online

The library's Local History Collection (50 South Fullerton Avenue) holds historic atlases (1890, 1906, 1933), the Montclair Times archive, vertical subject and house files, school yearbooks and photographs. Together with the Montclair History Center it maintains Montclair History Online on Digifind-IT, with digitized Montclair city directories from 1870 to 1969.

Montclair History Center & Historic Preservation Commission

Founded in 1965 to save the 1796 Israel Crane House, the Montclair History Center (108–110 Orange Road) maintains archives on Montclair houses, neighborhoods, the Historic YWCA and oral histories, and offers house-research guidance. The Township's Historic Preservation Commission publishes architectural surveys, the 2016 Historic Preservation Plan Element and local district guidelines.

Check whether your property sits in one of Montclair's National Register or local historic districts (Town Center, Upper Montclair, Mountain, Pine Street, Oakcroft, Watchung Plaza, Marlboro Park and more).

Step by step

How to research your Montclair house

A practical path through Montclair's public archives, atlases, directories and county records.

  1. Estimate the date

    Start with Montclair and Essex County tax assessment records. Treat the tax-card year as a starting clue—for pre-1900 houses it is often approximate. Roughly 60% of Montclair's homes were built in 1939 or earlier (median year built: 1938).

  2. Find your block & lot

    Your tax-map block and lot number unlock Essex County deeds, Township building permits and Montclair Historic Preservation Commission architectural survey forms.

  3. Walk through the city directories

    Montclair History Online (Digifind-IT) hosts Montclair city directories from 1870 to 1969. Look up your address in our 1910–1949 archive first, then check earlier and later volumes at Digifind-IT—and watch for street renumbering, especially before and after 1907.

  4. Read the Sanborn maps & county atlases

    The Library of Congress holds public-domain Sanborn fire-insurance maps of Montclair for 1885 (4 sheets) and 1907 (Essex County Vol. 3, 68 Montclair sheets) showing building footprints, porches, stables and materials. Pair them with the 1890 and 1906 Essex County atlases at the library and our 1888–1972 USGS map overlays.

  5. Check the historic surveys & districts

    Review the 1986 National Register Historic Resources of Montclair Multiple Property Submission and Montclair's 2016 Historic Preservation Plan Element. Even individual houses outside a district often appear in the 1980s statewide architectural survey.

  6. Trace the deeds

    Follow the grantor–grantee chain at the Essex County Register of Deeds & Mortgages in Newark. Remember that before April 15, 1868, Montclair deeds were recorded under Bloomfield Township (and before 1812 under Newark).

  7. Search the Montclair Times

    The Montclair Times (founded 1877) printed building permits, real-estate transfers, architects' announcements, weddings and obituaries—searchable at the Montclair Public Library Local History Room and online archives.

  8. Meet the people

    Federal census schedules (1870–1950, with Ancestry Library Edition free at Montclair Public Library) and Montclair High School yearbooks put families, occupations and stories to the names you uncover in the directories and deeds.

How this site works

Facts, stories & pictures

Facts. Every dated place and event comes from one of the sources listed below, and each card links to its source. Where sources disagree (for instance, on the exact founding dates of early Cranetown and Speertown farmhouses or the original 1873 depot sites), we note the discrepancy explicitly.

House records. House pages combine verified neighborhood histories with residents from the Montclair city directories (1910–1949 via Montclair History Online, strictly pre-1951 to protect living residents' privacy) and matching sheets of the public-domain 1885 and 1907 Sanborn fire-insurance maps from the Library of Congress. Notable people are linked to a Montclair address only if they are deceased and have a verified pre-1951 tie.

Stories. The "what it was like" passages and house narratives are our own narrative synthesis of those sourced facts. A house story describes the neighborhood and spot, not a deed history of a specific building.

Pictures. The Time Machine panorama is an artistic interpretation arranged for storytelling rather than strict geography. Landmark photographs are public-domain or Creative Commons images with author and license credits on every photo.

Boundaries and names. Before Montclair separated from Bloomfield Township on April 15, 1868, it was part of larger municipalities (Newark 1666–1812, Bloomfield 1812–1868). Because colonial-era boundary surveys do not survive in digital form, the Explore map builds pre-1868 outlines from modern U.S. Census TIGER municipal polygons and marks them approximate.

Not official. This is an unofficial fan site with no affiliation to the Township of Montclair, the Montclair Public Library or the Montclair History Center.

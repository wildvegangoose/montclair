Landmarks

The places that made Montclair

From the 1796 Israel Crane House and Speertown's sandstone Van Reyper-Bond House to the Shultz mansion, Olmsted-designed Anderson Park, the Carnegie Library, Lackawanna Terminal, Montclair Art Museum and Presby's hillside of irises: in-depth histories, timelines, and the same spot seen through USGS survey maps and aerial photos from 1888 to today.

1796
Federal Mansion & Historic YWCA

Israel Crane House & Historic YWCA

📍 110 Orange Road (moved from 159 Glenridge Ave in 1965)

Built by Cranetown's turnpike entrepreneur, home to enslaved workers and the Black YWCA for 45 years, and rolled a mile down the street in 1965.

1872
Italianate Farmhouse

Van Reyper-Bond House

📍 848 Valley Road

A 1872 Italianate farmhouse tying Speertown's founding Dutch settlers to the campus of Montclair State University.

1880s–1930s
Working-Class Brick Neighborhood

Pine Street Historic District

📍 Pine Street & Glenridge Avenue

Montclair's brick working-class heartland—built beside the railroad yards by Italian American and African American families.

1892 (rebuilt 2010)
Railroad Station

Upper Montclair Station

📍 275 Bellevue Avenue

The commuter gateway that turned rural Dutch Speertown into Upper Montclair village—reborn in stone after a 2006 fire.

1896
Victorian Mansion

Charles S. Shultz House ("Evergreens")

📍 30 North Mountain Avenue

A banker's fire-resistant Victorian showplace by the co-designer of Manhattan's Metropolitan Life Tower, lived in by one family for a century.

1901
Historic Firehouse

Cliffside Hose Company No. 4

📍 588 Valley Road

Upper Montclair's century-old neighborhood firehouse—still sending Engine 2 and Truck 2 out onto Valley Road.

1903–1909
Olmsted Neighborhood Park

Anderson Park

📍 274 Bellevue Avenue

Upper Montclair's "beautiful breathing space"—a 14.85-acre meadow designed by the Olmsted Brothers beside the train station.

1902–1904
Carnegie Library

Montclair Carnegie Library

📍 73 Church Street (at Valley Road)

A Beaux-Arts Carnegie library designed by the founder of UC Berkeley's School of Architecture.

1904 (line 1873)
Elevated Railroad Station

Watchung Avenue Station

📍 106 Watchung Avenue (at Park Street)

Montclair's only elevated rail station—perched on an embankment above Watchung Plaza since 1904.

1908
Historic University Hall

Montclair State Normal School (College Hall)

📍 1 Normal Avenue

Opened in 1908 as a two-year teacher-training school in Spanish Colonial Revival stucco—now the heart of New Jersey's second-largest university.

1913
Railroad Terminal

Lackawanna Railroad Terminal

📍 1 Lackawanna Plaza

A grand six-track Grecian-Doric terminal whose architect perished on the Titanic—and where Thomas Edison drove the first electric train in 1930.

1914
Art Museum

Montclair Art Museum

📍 3 South Mountain Avenue

New Jersey's first public art museum—born from Montclair's artists' colony and home to an unmatched George Inness and Native American art collection.

1916–1920 (congregation 1870)
Gothic Revival Church

First Congregational Church

📍 40–42 South Fullerton Avenue

A masterwork of American Perpendicular Gothic architecture by Bertram Grosvenor Goodhue, risen from the ashes of a 1914 fire.

1922
Historic Theater & Concert Hall

Wellmont Theater

📍 5 Seymour Street

From 1922 vaudeville palace and decades-long movie house to northern New Jersey's premier live-music and film-festival stage.

1927 (Walther House 1898)
Living Botanical Museum

Presby Memorial Iris Gardens

📍 474 Upper Mountain Avenue

Upper Montclair's "Rainbow on the Hill"—a living museum of thousands of historic iris varieties blooming beneath the 1898 Walther House.

1930
Georgian Revival Mansion

The Anchorage

📍 155 Wildwood Avenue

Upper Montclair's Virginia-inspired Georgian Revival showplace—built with James River clay bricks at the close of the estate era.

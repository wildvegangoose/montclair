Victorian Mansion · 1896

Charles S. Shultz House ("Evergreens")

A banker's fire-resistant Victorian showplace by the co-designer of Manhattan's Metropolitan Life Tower, lived in by one family for a century.

📍 30 North Mountain Avenue🏛 National Register of Historic Places (1979, #79001482)✏️ Michel M. LeBrun (Napoleon LeBrun & Sons)

Charles S. Shultz House ("Evergreens") at 30 North Mountain Avenue · Jrogers123 / Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 3.0

Built by a skyscraper architect

In 1896 Hoboken Savings Bank president Charles S. Shultz commissioned Montclair resident Michel M. LeBrun—of Napoleon LeBrun & Sons, famed for New York firehouses, the Hoboken Bank for Savings and later the 1909 Metropolitan Life Tower (briefly the world's tallest building)—to build a 40-foot-tall mansion surrounded by evergreen trees on North Mountain Avenue.

Drawing on European travels, LeBrun created an asymmetrical, eclectic Victorian house with an arched entrance hood, broad veranda and masonry first floor built specifically to resist fire.

Gilded Age technology and three generations

Shultz outfitted "Evergreens" with every late-19th-century convenience while engineering careful safeguards against them: combination gas-and-electric fixtures, modern plumbing, an electric burglar alarm, a room annunciator system, a built-in icebox and an interior elevator.

Remarkably, three generations of the Shultz family—including Charles, his son Clifford, daughter Emily and granddaughter Molly Shultz—lived here for nearly a century (regularly listed at 30 North Mountain Avenue in Montclair city directories from 1910 through 1949). Operated as a historic house museum by the Montclair History Center from 1997 to 2021, it returned to private residential ownership afterward.

Did you know?

  • Architect Michel LeBrun also helped choose Albert Randolph Ross to design the Montclair Art Museum down the street.
  • Montclair city directories confirm Charles S., Clifford G. and Emily B. Shultz living at 30 North Mountain Avenue from 1910 through 1949.
  • Added to the National Register of Historic Places in 1979 (#79001482).

Wellmont Theater

From 1922 vaudeville palace and decades-long movie house to northern New Jersey's premier live-music and film-festival stage.

📍 5 Seymour Street🏛 Downtown Montclair cultural landmark✏️ Reilly & Hall (1922); Brian Swier & Michael Costantin restoration (2008)

The Wellmont Theater marquee during the Montclair Film Festival · Ekem / Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 4.0

Vaudeville palace to neighborhood cinema

Designed by architects Reilly & Hall just off Bloomfield Avenue, the Wellmont Theater opened on June 16, 1922—right as Montclair's population surged past 30,000. Its first seven years brought live vaudeville, stage plays and silent-film bills to downtown Montclair.

In 1929, as sound cinema swept the country, the Wellmont converted into a dedicated motion-picture theater, later subdivided into a triplex where generations of Montclair teenagers—including future Palme d'Or–winning filmmaker Sean Baker, who worked as a Wellmont projectionist in high school in the 1980s—watched movies.

Revival as a concert hall and festival flagship

After years of decline as a multiplex, The Bowery Presents invested $3 million in a 2008 restoration designed by architects Brian Swier and Michael Costantin—removing the dividing walls to restore the grand single-auditorium orchestra and mezzanine.

Booked by Live Nation since 2013 and serving as a flagship venue for the annual Montclair Film Festival since 2012, the Wellmont has hosted B.B. King, Steely Dan, Ms. Lauryn Hill and hundreds of touring artists.

Did you know?

  • Opened June 16, 1922 as a live vaudeville and theater venue before switching to movies in 1929.

  • Restored in 2008 to a single grand concert hall and serves as a flagship venue of the Montclair Film Festival.

Israel Crane House & Historic YWCA

Built by Cranetown's turnpike entrepreneur, home to enslaved workers and the Black YWCA for 45 years, and rolled a mile down the street in 1965.

📍 110 Orange Road (moved from 159 Glenridge Ave in 1965)🏛 National Register of Historic Places (1973, #73001091)✏️ Israel Crane (Federal, 1796; Greek Revival alterations by James Crane)

Crane House & Historic YWCA at 110 Orange Road · Zeete / Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 4.0

Israel Crane's Federal mansion

In 1796, twenty-one-year-old Israel Crane built this four-level Federal mansion on Old Road—today's 159 Glenridge Avenue—near the heart of Cranetown. Crane became one of early Essex County's wealthiest entrepreneurs, financing the Newark–Pompton Turnpike (Bloomfield Avenue) while operating a general store, cider mill, distillery, textile mills and a Newark quarry.

Israel and his wife Fanny raised seven children in the house alongside hired servants and at least five enslaved people whose names survive in records: Bill, Joe, Jack, Bett and Dine. In the mid-1800s Israel's son James Crane remodeled the house with Greek Revival front columns and trim, employing Irish indentured and paid domestic workers.

The Glenridge Avenue YWCA (1920–1965)

In 1920 the Young Women's Christian Association of Montclair–North Essex purchased the house for its headquarters. The branch had been founded in 1912 by Alice Hooe Foster, the first African American woman to graduate from Montclair High School, during an era when YWCAs were racially segregated.

For 45 years the Glenridge Avenue YWCA served as a vital residence, employment bridge and social hub for African American women and girls arriving in Montclair. In 1965, needing larger quarters, the YWCA decided to build anew on its Glenridge Avenue lot—threatening the 1796 mansion with demolition.

A one-mile move and a wider story

Before the National Historic Preservation Act even existed, Montclair preservationists organized to buy the house and move it. On August 25, 1965, movers rolled the 1796 structure roughly one mile to 110 Orange Road—remarkably, land once owned by Israel's ancestor Jasper Crane—and founded what became the Montclair Historical Society.

Renamed the Montclair History Center in 2014, the campus also includes the 1818 Nathaniel Crane House and the 1894 James Henry Clark House, and now interprets both the Crane family era (including slavery) and the 45-year YWCA legacy side by side.

Did you know?

  • Moved about one mile from 159 Glenridge Avenue to 110 Orange Road on August 25, 1965.
  • Served for 45 years (1920–1965) as the segregated Glenridge Avenue YWCA for Black women and girls.
  • Its working hearth kitchen was reconstructed using 1937 Historic American Buildings Survey (HABS) drawings and photographs.
  • Added to the National Register of Historic Places on March 14, 1973.

Montclair State Normal School (College Hall)

Opened in 1908 as a two-year teacher-training school in Spanish Colonial Revival stucco—now the heart of New Jersey's second-largest university.

📍 1 Normal Avenue🏛 Original flagship building of Montclair State University✏️ Spanish Colonial Revival (1908)

College Hall at Montclair State University · Adam Moss / Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 2.0

A mission-style hilltop campus

Perched on the crest of First Watchung Mountain at Montclair's northern tip, the New Jersey State Normal School at Montclair welcomed its first students on September 28, 1908. Its inaugural building, College Hall, established the campus's signature Spanish Colonial Revival look—white stucco walls, red-tile roofs and arched loggias gazing east toward the Manhattan skyline.

Joined between 1908 and 1928 by Russ Hall, Chapin Hall and Morehead Hall, the young school trained generations of New Jersey public-school teachers.

From normal school to research university

The institution expanded in steady steps: becoming Montclair State Teachers College in 1927 (offering four-year degrees and master's programs by 1932), absorbing Panzer College of Physical Education and becoming Montclair State College in 1958, and acquiring former traprock quarry acreage between 1950 and 1980.

Elevated to Montclair State University in 1994—and connected directly to Manhattan when NJ Transit opened the Montclair State University rail station in October 2004—it has grown into New Jersey's second-largest university while keeping 1908 College Hall as its emblem.

Did you know?

  • Established September 28, 1908 as the New Jersey State Normal School at Montclair.
  • College Hall (1908) set the Spanish Colonial Revival architectural theme still echoed across campus.
  • Became Montclair State Teachers College (1927), Montclair State College (1958) and Montclair State University (1994).

Carnegie Library · 1902–1904

Montclair Carnegie Library

A Beaux-Arts Carnegie library designed by the founder of UC Berkeley's School of Architecture.

📍 73 Church Street (at Valley Road)🏛 National Register of Historic Places (1988, #86003074)✏️ John Galen Howard

Former Montclair Carnegie Library at Church Street and Valley Road · Jerrye & Roy Klotz MD / Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 4.0

A California master's New Jersey gem

Montclair's Free Public Library—one of the oldest in New Jersey—secured an Andrew Carnegie building grant at the turn of the century. To design the new library at Church Street and Valley Road, trustees turned to architect John Galen Howard, fresh off designing the Electric Tower for the 1901 Pan-American Exposition and on the cusp of moving west to design UC Berkeley's classical campus (where he also gave pioneering architect Julia Morgan her first American architectural post).

Completed between 1902 and 1904, Howard's symmetrical brick-and-limestone building brought Beaux-Arts polish to the western end of Church Street.

Half a century of books and adaptive reuse

The Church Street Carnegie building served as Montclair's main library for more than fifty years—complemented from 1914 by a second Carnegie branch on Bellevue Avenue in Upper Montclair—until the library opened a larger modern headquarters at 50 South Fullerton Avenue in 1955.

Added to the National Register of Historic Places in 1988, the former library has been preserved through adaptive reuse as the Montclair Kimberley Academy Middle School campus and civic space.

Did you know?

  • Designed by John Galen Howard (1902–1904), architect of Sather Tower and Doe Library at UC Berkeley.
  • Served as Montclair's main public library until 1955.

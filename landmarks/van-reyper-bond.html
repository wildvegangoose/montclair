Italianate Farmhouse · 1872

Van Reyper-Bond House

A 1872 Italianate farmhouse tying Speertown's founding Dutch settlers to the campus of Montclair State University.

📍 848 Valley Road🏛 National Register of Historic Places (1979, #79001483)✏️ Thomas C. Van Reyper (Italianate)

Van Reyper-Bond House on Valley Road · Adam Moss / Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 2.0

Speertown's rural heritage

Built in 1872 along northern Valley Road—once Speertown Road—the Van Reyper-Bond House recalls the centuries when northern Montclair was a Dutch farming village. Thomas C. Van Reyper designed and built the Italianate residence for himself and his wife, Caroline Speer, a descendant of the Dutch Speer family who gave Speertown its name.

Standing just one year before the Montclair Railway opened nearby Montclair Heights station, the house marks the exact transition between Montclair's agricultural past and its suburban future.

From Bond family home to university landmark

Later owned by the Bond family, the 1.4-acre property was eventually absorbed into the expanding campus of Montclair State University (which began just across Normal Avenue as the New Jersey State Normal School in 1908).

Added to the National Register of Historic Places on January 22, 1979, the restored house remains one of the finest surviving 19th-century farmhouses in northern Essex County.

Did you know?

  • Thomas C. Van Reyper designed the house himself in the Italianate style in 1872.
  • His wife, Caroline Speer, descended from the Dutch settlers who founded Speertown (Upper Montclair).
  • Added to the National Register of Historic Places on January 22, 1979 (#79001483).

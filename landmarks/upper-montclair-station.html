Upper Montclair Station

The commuter gateway that turned rural Dutch Speertown into Upper Montclair village—reborn in stone after a 2006 fire.

📍 275 Bellevue Avenue🏛 Originally NRHP (1984); rebuilt after 2006 fire✏️ Erie Railroad (1892 stone depot); reconstructed 2010

How a railroad remade Speertown

Montclair broke away from Bloomfield in 1868 largely to finance the Montclair Railway through its northern farms. When trains began stopping here in 1873, rural Speertown rapidly became "Upper Montclair," with Bellevue Avenue and Valley Road blossoming into a bustling shopping district beside the tracks.

In 1892 the Erie Railroad replaced the earlier wooden shelter with a picturesque Richardsonian Romanesque stone depot—pairing heavy ashlar masonry with a broad overhanging roof that anchored the village for more than a century.

Midtown Direct, fire and faithful reconstruction

In 1983 Montclair leased all six of its rail stations from NJ Transit, and in 1984 the 1892 Upper Montclair depot joined the National Register of Historic Places. On September 30, 2002, the Montclair Connection brought direct electric service to New York Penn Station.

In 2006 a severe fire gutted the historic 1892 station house. Rather than settle for a utilitarian shelter, NJ Transit and a private restaurant partner reconstructed the depot in its original historic style—reopening in June 2010 with both commuter waiting rooms and a restaurant overlooking the tracks and Anderson Park.

Did you know?

  • Rail service to Upper Montclair began in 1873; the stone Erie depot opened in 1892.
  • Sits directly across Bellevue Avenue from Olmsted-designed Anderson Park.
  • Reopened in June 2010 after a $1 million reconstruction following the 2006 fire.

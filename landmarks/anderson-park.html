Olmsted Neighborhood Park · 1903–1909

Anderson Park

Upper Montclair's "beautiful breathing space"—a 14.85-acre meadow designed by the Olmsted Brothers beside the train station.

📍 274 Bellevue Avenue🏛 National Register of Historic Places (2009, #09001073)✏️ Olmsted Brothers (John Charles Olmsted & Frederick Law Olmsted Jr.)

The sweeping central lawn of Anderson Park · Wikimedia Commons contributor · CC BY-SA 3.0

Charles W. Anderson's gift

In 1902 New York insurance executive and Upper Montclair developer Charles W. Anderson offered nearly 15 acres of open land at Bellevue and North Mountain Avenues to Montclair on condition that it become part of the new Essex County Park System—spurring voters to approve a $1 million countywide park bond in 1903.

Initially called Montclair Park, it was officially renamed Anderson Park in 1909 at the town's request. Anderson and his son Robert also shaped the Tudor Revival architecture of adjacent Upper Montclair, while the park's arrival fueled the surrounding Oakcroft residential subdivision.

An intact Olmsted Brothers design

John Charles Olmsted and Frederick Law Olmsted Jr. (stepson and son of Central Park co-creator Frederick Law Olmsted Sr.) designed Anderson Park as the quintessential suburban "neighborhood park"—a sweeping central greensward ringed by curved walking paths and shade trees, tucked directly beside the commuter station and village shops.

Added to the National Register of Historic Places in 2009, Anderson Park retains its original early-20th-century layout and inspired subsequent Olmsted commissions across Montclair, including Edgemont Memorial Park, Glenfield Park and Brookdale Park.

Did you know?

  • Donated by Charles W. Anderson in 1903 and renamed Anderson Park in 1909.
  • Designed by the Olmsted Brothers of Brookline, Massachusetts.
  • Its original 1903 plan already included southern entrances anticipating the Oakcroft neighborhood before those streets were laid out.
  • Added to the National Register of Historic Places in 2009 (#09001073).

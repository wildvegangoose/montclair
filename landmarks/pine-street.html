Working-Class Brick Neighborhood · 1880s–1930s

Pine Street Historic District

Montclair's brick working-class heartland—built beside the railroad yards by Italian American and African American families.

📍 Pine Street & Glenridge Avenue🏛 National Register of Historic Places (2000)

Historic brick apartment and commercial blocks along Pine Street · Magicpiano / Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 4.0

The other side of suburban Montclair

While Montclair's western ridges filled with detached estates, the flat blocks around Pine Street, Glenridge Avenue, Bay Street and Grant Street grew into an urban, multi-family neighborhood tied to the nearby Lackawanna freight yards, coal depots, dairies and small factories.

Between the 1880s and the 1930s builders lined these narrow streets with three- and four-story Classical Revival, Renaissance Revival and Italianate brick buildings—such as 78–80 Pine Street (c. 1912) and the buff-brick apartments at 70 Pine Street (c. 1924)—with corner bakeries, barbershops and social clubs on the ground floors.

Immigrant and Black community anchor

Pine Street became the historic center of Montclair's Italian American community and a closely shared neighborhood with African American residents living along nearby Glenridge Avenue, Mission Street and Bloomfield Avenue. City directories from 1910 to 1949 capture generations of masons, tailors, barbers, laborers and shopkeepers living side by side on these blocks.

Added to the National Register of Historic Places in 2000, the district preserves an irreplaceable urban counterweight to Montclair's estate districts. The c. 1890 house at 17 Grant Street was also recorded by the Historic American Buildings Survey in 1996.

Did you know?

  • Built largely between 1880 and the 1930s in Italianate, Renaissance Revival and Classical Revival brick.
  • Sits steps from today's Bay Street NJ Transit station and the 1913 Lackawanna Terminal.
  • 17 Grant Street (c. 1890) was documented by the Historic American Buildings Survey (HABS) in 1996.

Montclair Art Museum

New Jersey's first public art museum—born from Montclair's artists' colony and home to an unmatched George Inness and Native American art collection.

Born from an artists' colony

By 1900 Montclair had drawn a remarkable colony of painters, sculptors and collectors—led by landscape master George Inness, who lived on Grove Street from 1885 until 1894. In 1909 dry-goods merchant and art patron William T. Evans offered 54 American paintings and sculptures to Montclair if the town would house them; fellow resident Florence Rand Lang stepped forward with funding for the building and a major collection of Native North American art built with her mother, Annie Valentine Rand.

Trustee Michel LeBrun engaged architect Albert Randolph Ross to design a dignified Neoclassical temple at Bloomfield and South Mountain Avenues. When the Montclair Art Museum opened on January 15, 1914, it was New Jersey's first public museum dedicated solely to art—and one of the first museums in America devoted to both American and Native American art.

A century of expansion

Gifts from Florence Rand Lang funded the front Ionic portico and mezzanine in 1924 and an East Wing in 1931 to house the Rand Collection of Native art, which now spans thousands of historical and contemporary Indigenous works. Outside the entrance stood Hermon Atkins MacNeil's bronze sculpture The Sun Vow, gifted by Evans.

Added to the National Register of Historic Places on November 14, 1986, the museum doubled in size in 2000–2001 through a renovation by Beyer Blinder Belle and remains home to the only gallery anywhere dedicated exclusively to George Inness.

Did you know?

  • Opened January 15, 1914—the first public museum in New Jersey dedicated solely to art.
  • Founded through gifts from Montclair residents William T. Evans and Florence Rand Lang.
  • Holds over 12,000 objects, including a dedicated gallery of 21 paintings by Montclair resident George Inness and ~4,000 Native North American works.
  • Added to the National Register of Historic Places on November 14, 1986.

First Congregational Church

A masterwork of American Perpendicular Gothic architecture by Bertram Grosvenor Goodhue, risen from the ashes of a 1914 fire.

📍 40–42 South Fullerton Avenue🏛 National Register of Historic Places (1988)✏️ Bertram Grosvenor Goodhue (Perpendicular Gothic)

First Congregational Church on South Fullerton Avenue · Jim.henderson / Wikimedia Commons · CC0

Rebuilt after a 1914 fire

Organized in 1870 just two years after Montclair separated from Bloomfield, the First Congregational Church built its original sanctuary on South Fullerton Avenue. When a devastating fire destroyed that building in 1914, the congregation turned to Bertram Grosvenor Goodhue—one of America's foremost Gothic Revival architects, celebrated for St. Thomas Church and St. Bartholomew's in Manhattan and the Nebraska State Capitol.

Completed in 1916–1920, Goodhue's stone church blends soaring Perpendicular Gothic tracery, buttressed limestone walls and an intimately proportioned parish complex.

Architectural and musical heritage

Architectural historian Eleanor Price wrote in the church's National Register nomination that it stands as "one of the finest examples of Gothic church architecture in the country." From 1917 to 1939 British-born composer and organist Mark Andrews led its renowned music program.

Listed on the National Register of Historic Places on July 1, 1988 in Montclair's Multiple Property Submission, First Congregational remains a civic and cultural anchor steps from Bloomfield Avenue and the Montclair Public Library.

Did you know?

  • Designed by Bertram Grosvenor Goodhue in the Late Gothic Revival / Perpendicular Gothic style.
  • Replaced an earlier First Congregational sanctuary destroyed by fire in 1914.
  • British-born composer Mark Andrews served as church organist from 1917 to 1939.
  • Added to the National Register of Historic Places on July 1, 1988.
